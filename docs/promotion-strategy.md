# Off-site promotion strategy

Written 2026-07-26, following the SEO audit's GEO finding: near-zero search presence for
"Sector 4" F1, and the name collides with generic "sector" content plus F1's own
S1–S4 sector terminology. This doc is the plan; copy drafts for the top channels are in
`docs/promotion-copy.md`.

## Reality check first

- **Confirmed via live search:** zero relevant results for `"Sector 4" F1` or
  `sector4.net`. A competing site, [sector.world](https://sector.world) ("your home for
  news and intelligence on the business of Formula 1"), already occupies adjacent
  positioning under a similar name — worth knowing about, not just an SEO nuisance.
- **This is a brand-new, small, pre-revenue project.** The strategy below is scoped to
  that reality — no PR agency, no ad spend, no existing audience. Channels that need
  either don't make the list.

## Recommended (in priority order)

1. **A Show HN post (news.ycombinator.com).** Best fit: HN's audience skews toward
   exactly the people who'd appreciate "we ran the ML validation and honestly reported
   what didn't work" (PRD §5.1's grid/standings-beat-telemetry finding) rather than a
   confident-sounding pitch. Post once, engage in comments, don't resubmit.
2. **A small/niche F1-adjacent community, not r/formula1 directly.** r/formula1 is a
   multi-million-member subreddit with strict, actively-enforced anti-self-promotion
   rules (mod-approval-only for anything promotional, is the norm for subreddits at that
   size) — I can't fetch its current rule wiki to confirm the exact text, so verify
   directly before posting rather than trusting this doc. Better first move: a smaller
   strategy/stats-focused F1 community (e.g. r/F1Technical, which explicitly welcomes
   data/analysis content) or a relevant Discord server, where a genuine "I built this,
   here's the honest validation behind it" post reads as a contribution, not spam.
3. **A personal/project X (Twitter) account**, posting screenshots + the honesty angle
   (the `/accuracy` page, the "grid beats telemetry" finding) rather than generic hype.
   Low effort to start, compounds slowly, no algorithm risk of a single bad post tanking
   reach the way a subreddit ban would.
4. **Product Hunt**, once there's something demo-able end to end. Lower priority than
   the above — PH traffic skews toward SaaS-tool evaluators, a narrower fit than HN/F1
   communities for this specific audience, but it's a real, free, no-account-friction
   listing worth doing eventually.

## Explicitly NOT recommended

- **Wikipedia.** A brand-new, small, single-person project has no realistic path past
  Wikipedia's notability bar (independent, reliable secondary source coverage) — an
  article would very likely be deleted (WP:CSD/WP:AFD), and attempting one before that
  coverage exists reads as promotional, which is itself against Wikipedia's rules for
  the person creating it. Skip until/unless real independent press coverage exists.
- **Reddit mass-posting across multiple F1 subreddits at once.** Reads as spam,
  triggers ban/shadowban risk, and burns the one good post you'd otherwise get to make
  well later.
- **Buying followers/engagement, backlink-farm submissions, or "SEO directory"
  submissions.** Low-quality backlinks are worth roughly nothing to modern search
  ranking and carry real spam-signal risk.
